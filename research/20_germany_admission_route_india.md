# Germany: how an Indian 12th-grade (HSC) student gets into a Bachelor
Sources: APS India news 23 Feb 2026 https://aps-india.de/news/ ; DAAD India https://www.daad.in/en/study-research-in-germany/studying-in-germany/bachelor-studies/ ; DAAD requirements https://www.daad.de/en/studying-in-germany/requirements/overview/ ; DAAD brochure https://www.daad.in/files/2022/08/Bachelor_IBN_2026-1.pdf
Accessed 2026-10-24. Confidence: HIGH (official).

## Rules
- Indian Class 12 / HSC (any board) does NOT give direct entry. Options:
  1. Studienkolleg (1-year foundation, usually 2 semesters, in GERMANY, TAUGHT IN GERMAN) + Feststellungsprüfung (FSP). Needs ~B1/B2 German (approx B2) + entrance exam (Aufnahmeprüfung, in Germany). Passing FSP only makes you eligible; you still apply to university. => this is why nephew was learning German.
  2. One successfully completed academic year of a recognised Indian Bachelor's (subject-related) + Class XII >= 70% + APS certificate -> direct subject-restricted admission.
  3. Valid JEE Advanced result (technology) = direct eligibility (DAAD).
  4. IB diploma / A-levels etc.
- From Winter Semester 2026/27: Class XII must be >= 70% overall (any board). Nephew 82% OK (assumed >80% overall).
- APS certificate (Academic Evaluation Centre, Delhi) mandatory for visa/admission.
- Exceptions: some PRIVATE universities (e.g. SRH: "applicants with foreign degrees might be eligible for direct entry... don't need foundation year" - check Indian HSC specifically) and Constructor Univ (SAT-based, TODO verify) can admit directly but charge tuition.

## Time cost
- Studienkolleg route: ~1 year of Studienkolleg (T-Kurs for tech) + 6-7 sem bachelor (THI/DIT/OTH are 7 semesters = 3.5 yrs) => ~4.5-5 years total before earning; plus prior German learning (B2 takes ~600-800 h; he's partly done).
- Route 2 (1 yr in India first): cost-effective? Indian bachelor first year (CS) then transfer = ~1 yr delay but no German-language Studienkolleg. But must enrol in a recognised Indian B.Tech/BSc and "successfully complete" the year. Then apply to English-taught bachelors (admissions like THI open).
- Important nuance: Studienkolleg teaching is German-language; but bachelor itself can be English. So German language is needed for (a) Studienkolleg and (b) living/part-time jobs/internships and (c) most good jobs after graduation, even if the course is English.

## Why this matters
- The nephew had prepared for exactly this (learning German) and then abandoned it because Germany "felt dry/DIY". The admission route is slow and language-heavy; Japan's ISSE is more direct (English-only, direct admission with DET). Fair pivot on logistics even if not on culture.
- A quicker German alternative: 1 year Indian bachelor (or even JEE Advanced if he sat it) -> direct admission.
