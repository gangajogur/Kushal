## Costs (public route example: THI)
- Tuition: EUR 800 x 7 = EUR 5,600 + semester fees ~EUR 100-200 x 7 (~EUR 1,000) => ~EUR 6.6k.
- Living: EUR 900-1,200/mo (DAAD); Ingolstadt/Regensburg ~EUR 800-1,100. Blocked account EUR 992/mo = EUR 11,904/yr proof (2026). Health insurance EUR 120-150/mo. Work: students may work 140 full days/120 half-days a year (general knowledge; verify), typical Werkstudent EUR 14-16/hr.
- 3.5 yr living ~EUR 35-40k. Studienkolleg year (public: low tuition but living ~EUR 12-14k + German learning cost).
- TOTAL (public, via Studienkolleg) ~ EUR 55-65k (~Rs 55-65 lakh at ~EUR1=Rs100) - this is the cheapest of the three destinations by far. (VERIFY EUR rate.)
- Private (Constructor): ~EUR 70k tuition (3 yr, pre-scholarship) + living EUR 12k/yr x3 => ~EUR 105-110k pre-scholarship (~Rs 1.1 crore).

## Post-study (not yet researched in depth)
- 18-month job-seeking residence permit after graduation; EU Blue Card; salary thresholds (TODO verify 2026). German is practically required for living/finding the first job, though English-only roles exist in big cities.
- German games industry: Gamescom (Cologne), studios Crytek, Yager, Ubisoft Blue Byte, InnoGames, Wooga etc. (general knowledge; TODO verify) - small, English work language at some, many studios lay-off-prone.

## Verdict on Germany for THIS nephew
- Pros: cheapest by far; AI curricula strong and modern (THI includes dialogue agents/chatbots, multi-agent systems -> matches his chatbot/NPC interest); 7-semester degree with internship options; EU access.
- Cons: HSC => Studienkolleg (German B2, in Germany) adds ~1 yr; tiny/rural campuses (Ingolstadt, Deggendorf) + "DIY" culture = the exact thing he disliked; only 1-2 truly game-specific English programmes and they are design/arts-heavy (B.A.), with aptitude tests; no C++/graphics in AI programmes.
- Best way if pursuing Germany: (a) do 1 year of Indian bachelor's in CS (or JEE route) -> direct admission to THI/OTH/h_da etc. -> skips Studienkolleg; or (b) go via Studienkolleg and then THI/DIT; (c) private Constructor for campus life if loan-funded is acceptable.
