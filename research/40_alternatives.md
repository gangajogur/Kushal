# Non-university and alternative approaches (pros/cons)
Evidence base: files 09, 10, 12, 21, 50. Some items are general knowledge and flagged.

## 1. Self-study + portfolio (no degree)
Examples: CS50, Nand2Tetris, Game Programming Patterns, LearnOpenGL, Handmade Hero-style C/C++, Godot/Unreal, Sutton & Barto + Hugging Face Deep RL, Karpathy's LLM lectures, Game AI Pro, game jams (itch.io, Global Game Jam), open-source contributions, building LLM-NPC prototypes with memory (his SillyTavern hit this).
Pros: ~Rs 2-4 lakh; moves at AI speed; his stated pain (context/memory wall) is exactly the thing he can attack directly; portfolio is what Game Freak/Capcom evaluate (Game Freak: school/major irrelevant, portfolio + practical test).
Cons: no visa pathway (a bachelor's is usually required for Japan/Germany/Australia work visas - Japan work visa generally needs a degree or 10 yrs experience; general knowledge, verify), no cohort/network, discipline risk, weak maths if skipped, credential filters in Western HR.
Verdict: essential COMPLEMENT, not a substitute for someone who wants to work abroad.

## 2. Vocational games diplomas (AIE, SAE, Japan senmon gakko, Digital Hollywood)
Pros: games-specific, C++/engines, portfolio, short (2 yrs), team projects with artists.
Cons: ~AUD 65k for 2 yrs at AIE; diploma not degree; weaker brand; Plan B outside games is poor; visa/post-study rights weaker; Japan senmon gakko are Japanese-language and visa requires N2-ish (verify).
Verdict: poor value for him: he is maths/AI-oriented, not art/design, and needs a degree for visas.

## 3. Online degrees / MOOCs (University of London online CS BSc, Coursera/edX certificates, OMSCS-type Master's)
Pros: cheap (University of London online BSc roughly 1/5 the price; general knowledge, verify), flexible, real degree.
Cons: no campus life/visa; employer perception variable; discipline. OMSCS-type Master's (~US$8-10k) is a very cheap AI/game-AI route AFTER a bachelor's.
Verdict: strong "Plan C" for a later Master's; for undergrad it fails his campus-life/visa aims.

## 4. Stay in India: B.Tech CSE (NIT/IIIT/private) + portfolio, then Master's abroad
Pros: cheapest by far (Rs 8-30 lakh), family nearby, parallel self-study; Master's abroad (Germany tuition-free-ish, Japan with MEXT/uni scholarships, Australia/US with research) is where the specialisation, visa and employer value are; Master's is also how Japan's engineering/R&D roles are entered (50% of Ritsumeikan CISE grads go to grad school).
Cons: depends on JEE/CET result and college tier (unknown); generic curriculum; less independence/adventure; 4 yrs + 2 yrs = 6 yrs to a Master's; he said he doesn't want to grind a Master's - but this route makes it cheap and funded.
Verdict: best financial hedge. Worth keeping as the "base case" to beat.

## 5. Hybrid: 1 year Indian bachelor's -> Germany public (skip Studienkolleg)
Pros: direct subject-restricted entry (70% HSC + APS + 1 successful bachelor year), cheaper than Studienkolleg, lets him test CS and German pace; THI/OTH/h_da/TH Koeln have English programmes.
Cons: German "DIY" culture he disliked; still German language needed to live well; the Indian year must be a recognised bachelor and "successfully completed".

## 6. Pivot options inside universities
- Deakin with scholarship >=50% or UWA are only worth it if the goal shifts from games to general software/AI jobs in English-speaking markets (and the family accepts Rs 140-170 lakh).
- Constructor (Germany): best campus life of the German options; costly.

## AI-era durability: what to hedge for
- Coding syntax and boilerplate are being commoditised; entry-level postings are shrinking and demanding "senior" traits (system design, communication, verification) - IZA, SSRN 2026.
- Durable: maths/ML foundations, systems/performance thinking, evaluating AI output, building end-to-end projects, domain knowledge (games, simulation, robotics), communication/team skills, languages (Japanese/German gives scarcity).
- Games-specific risk: layoffs (28% in 2 yrs), 8% entry-level share, 52% of devs view genAI negatively; but game-AI + LLM-agent skills transfer to non-games (agents, simulation, robotics, edtech).
