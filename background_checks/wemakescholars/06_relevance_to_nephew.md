# 06 - Relevance to the Nephew's Decision

Context (from `02_nephew_profile.md`, `04_progress_log.md`, `report/AUSTRALIA_DEAKIN_QUT.md`, `report/INDIA_VS_OVERSEAS_ASSESSMENT.md`): Indian student (Solapur, Maharashtra), Bachelor's. As of 2026-11-01 Ritsumeikan ISSE rejected him; live options are India-first (base case), Deakin / QUT (Australia, ~Rs 122-154 lakh all-in), Aizu (Japan, admission rules tightened), plus scholarship routes. Germany pivoted away from. Cost-conscious; a loan is acceptable to the family. Australia is by far the most loan-dependent of these, so loan-advice quality matters most there.

## Where WMS could matter
1. **Funding a Bachelor's overseas** requires a loan or family funds. WMS positions itself as the loan-and-scholarship channel.
2. **Scholarship search** - some value for discovery. Most Bachelor's scholarships for Australia/Japan sit on university sites and MEXT/JASSO/JICA-type official channels already covered in `research/03`, `13`, `14` and `07`. Use WMS only as a lead generator.

## Facts that apply to a Bachelor's loan (not WMS-specific)
- **Co-applicant is mandatory** at SBI (parent/guardian as co-borrower) and Credila ("mandatory; income and credit profile reviewed"). Nephew has no income, so family's credit/income decides everything. NBFCs offering "no co-applicant" loans (e.g. EliteScholars-style) mainly target US STEM/MBA, not Bachelor's abroad.
- **SBI Global Ed-Vantage** covers Australia, Japan and many European countries; undergraduate degrees eligible ("job oriented professional/technical Graduation Degree" wording on SBI page; confirm that Deakin CS / Ritsumeikan ISSE qualify as such with the branch).
- SBI collateral: up to INR 7.5 lakh only parent co-borrower, no collateral; above that, tangible collateral (SBI student-loan page). BankBazaar's summary of Ed-Vantage says collateral-free to INR 50 lakh for select institutions; **confirm which rule applies**.
- Typical Bachelor's totals (per `research/50_cost_model.md` and `51`): well above INR 7.5 lakh for Australia, lower for Japan with scholarships. So collateral or NBFC risk is likely if borrowing.
- Interest during study (PSI / simple interest during moratorium) and repayment start 12 months after course, up to 15 years (SBI).

## WMS-specific implications
- Bachelor's applicants are eligible for HEST and Education Future (Bachelors listed), but only if the **loan runs through WMS** and the student is not self-funding. Award size (up to INR 3 lakh / 2-10 lakh) is small against Australia's multi-lakh annual cost, and selection is competitive (~10,000+ HEST applicants). Not a basis for choosing a lender.
- If the family can fund fully or via a **public-bank loan applied directly**, they lose nothing except access to those two WMS-linked awards.
- No Japan- or Australia-specific WMS user feedback found. WMS has a dedicated Germany loan page (read); its homepage lists USA, UK, Canada, Australia, Germany as destinations. I did not check whether it has a Japan page or whether its lenders finance Japan Bachelor's programmes in practice (SBI's scheme does list Japan).
- Users' Germany threads (blocked account loans) are no longer relevant to the shortlist.

## Suggested use
- **Recommended path:** (1) Direct SBI / Bank of Baroda quote via branch and Vidya Lakshmi; (2) optionally one WMS conversation to benchmark, with all points in `05` checklist, (3) never pay a lender fee before written terms; (4) decide loan size after the cost model and any university scholarship outcomes (see `research/03`, `13`).
- If family needs a no-collateral option, price Credila/Avanse directly and via WMS and compare APR and fees side by side.
- Link this check to `report/INDIA_VS_OVERSEAS_ASSESSMENT.md`: debt burden and repayment risk is a key factor in the India-vs-overseas call; commission-driven advice should not be the input to that call.

## Open questions to ask the family / nephew
- Parent income, existing liabilities and CIBIL (determines co-applicant strength).
- Any property available for collateral?
- Planned loan size and whether parents prefer public bank only.
- Whether they have already submitted details to WMS or any lender (credit enquiries).
