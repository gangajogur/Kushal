# 05 - Questions to Ask Credila (Detailed)

Ask for answers **by email** or as annexes to the sanction letter. Verbal “starting from 9.95%” is marketing, not a commitment.

**How to use**
1. Fill in university, intake, co-applicant income, and whether property is available.
2. Send **1–6** first (price the loan).
3. Send **7–10** next (visa / timeline).
4. Send **11–15** before signing (trapdoors and exit).
5. Keep a parallel **SBI / BoB** quote on the **same** fee sheet.

Acronyms: see `04_acronyms.md`.

---

## Pricing & structure

### 1. For [uni + course + intake], with co-applicant income ₹X and [with/without] collateral ₹Y: what floating rate, CBLR spread, and reset frequency?

- **Why:** “From 9.95%” is the best case for someone else’s profile. Kushal’s rate depends on parents’ income/credit, collateral, uni, and UG vs PG.
- **Ask them to state:** e.g. “CBLR 9.0% + spread 2.5% = 11.5% floating, reset quarterly.”
- **Good answer:** Specific % for *this* profile; how often the rate can change; what happens if market rates fall.
- **Bad answer:** “Around 10–11%, we’ll confirm later” with no spread/reset detail.

### 2. Max sanction secured vs unsecured? Can Australia’s visa living minimum be included in full?

- **Why:** Australia all-in is ~₹1.5 cr. Unsecured often tops out near ~₹1 cr (profile-dependent), so QUT/Deakin may need collateral. Visa/GS needs proof of tuition **plus** living (and travel).
- **Good answer:** Clear max for both paths; written list of what living/travel they fund; whether living goes to student account or only tuition to uni.
- **Bad answer:** “Up to 100% finance” with no rupee cap or living-cost detail.

### 3. Exact origination fee % + GST; when payable; refundable if we reject the sanction?

- **Why:** Fee can be ~1%+ of a huge loan (lakhs). Complaints exist of fees taken **before** a signed agreement, then hard to refund.
- **Good answer:** Exact %, GST, debit date, and “fully refundable if sanction not accepted within X days.”
- **Bad answer:** Fee collected to “start processing” with no refund clause.

### 4. Mandatory vs optional insurance; premiums; added to principal?

- **Why:** Borrowers report life + travel/medical bundles of ₹40k–1.5L+. If added to principal, you pay interest on insurance for years.
- **Good answer:** Itemised list; each marked mandatory/optional; pay separately vs add to loan; cooling-off/cancel terms.
- **Bad answer:** “Insurance is required for approval” with no breakdown, or pressure to buy group policies same day.

### 5. During the 3-year course: SI, PSI amount, or both? Sample schedule if we pay only PSI (show capitalisation).

- **Why:** Family must budget monthly interest for ~3 years. PSI means the loan balance can **grow** before EMIs — QUT models already show heavy EMIs (`../../Loan/`).
- **Good answer:** Exact monthly SI/PSI for the proposed amount; a year-by-year table: paid interest, unpaid interest added, outstanding balance at course end.
- **Bad answer:** “Moratorium, no worry until after studies” (misleading for Credila-style PSI loans).

### 6. When does full EMI start after course end? Max tenure for UG Australia / Malaysia?

- **Why:** Some lenders allow course + 6–12 months grace. Tenure (15 vs 20 years for UG) changes EMI size a lot. APU vs Australia may differ.
- **Good answer:** “EMI starts course end + N months”; max tenure for Bachelor’s in AU and MY in writing.
- **Bad answer:** Vague “up to 15 years” with no UG-specific confirmation.

## Process & visa timing

### 7. Conditional sanction on offer letter alone (for GS/visa)? What else for final disbursement?

- **Why:** Visa/GS often needs strong financial evidence **before** or alongside lodging. Conditional sanction helps; final money still needs admission confirmation, invoices, KYC, etc.
- **Good answer:** Yes, conditional letter with amount/rate; checklist of remaining conditions; how long the letter stays valid.
- **Bad answer:** “Only after visa” or “only after full docs” with no interim letter you can show immigration.

### 8. Disbursement to university only, or living to student account? Tranche vs invoice?

- **Why:** If only tuition is paid to uni, family still needs cash for rent/food unless living is disbursed. Multi-year degrees need year-2/3 tranches timed to fee due dates.
- **Good answer:** Who gets paid; living amount and account; tranche calendar tied to invoices; FX handling for AUD invoices.
- **Bad answer:** Unclear living cover; “we disburse when we can.”

### 9. Written SLA for first and later-year disbursements; named RM + grievance path.

- **Why:** Common complaint: year-2 fees late, uni blocks enrolment, RM unresponsive, tickets closed with no fix.
- **Good answer:** “X working days after complete docs”; named RM; L1/L2 grievance emails; RBI ombudsman path if needed.
- **Bad answer:** No timeline; “call customer care”; office A cannot see accounts from office B with no escalation.

### 10. Full document list upfront for salaried vs self-employed co-applicant.

- **Why:** Biggest delay pattern is serial “send one more document.” Self-employed parents (ITR, audited books, office proof) get hit hardest.
- **Good answer:** One complete checklist by income type; “if these are complete, no further docs except valuation (if secured).”
- **Bad answer:** Open-ended “we’ll tell you as we go.”

## Collateral, credit & exit

### 11. If property is pledged: valuation, legal fees, who pays, release after full repayment?

- **Why:** Secured loans add cost and time (legal/technical valuation). Family must know when the property charge is removed after the loan is cleared.
- **Good answer:** Fee schedule, who pays, typical days, release process after foreclosure/full payment.
- **Bad answer:** Surprise legal bills; vague release timeline.

### 12. Will this enquiry do a hard CIBIL pull? Soft vs hard; how many?

- **Why:** Shopping multiple lenders with many **hard** pulls can hurt the co-applicant’s score and future loan pricing. Prefer soft check first, one hard pull when ready to proceed.
- **Good answer:** Soft eligibility first; hard pull only at application with consent; they tell you when it hits the report.
- **Bad answer:** Multiple hard pulls without warning.

### 13. Confirm zero prepayment/foreclosure charges; can we part-pay during study?

- **Why:** Website says no charges — lock that in the agreement. Extra payments during study can reduce capitalised interest and total cost.
- **Good answer:** Clause in sanction/loan agreement; part-pay allowed anytime; how to instruct it.
- **Bad answer:** Marketing says zero fees but agreement has conditions/lock-in.

### 14. Top-up if AUD strengthens or fees rise mid-course?

- **Why:** AUD/INR moves and uni fees rise. A 3-year degree can need more rupees than year-1 sanction.
- **Good answer:** Top-up eligibility, typical timing, rate on top-up, docs needed.
- **Bad answer:** “Not available” with no alternative (family funds / second lender).

### 15. How does your best Credila offer compare to SBI Global Ed-Vantage for the same course?

- **Why:** Forces a cost conversation. They may downplay banks — still useful. Then **verify at an SBI/BoB branch** yourself.
- **Good answer:** Honest rate/fee/collateral comparison, or “we don’t quote competitors — here’s our full APR breakdown.”
- **Bad answer:** “Banks are too slow / won’t approve” without numbers, while pushing you to pay Credila fees immediately.
