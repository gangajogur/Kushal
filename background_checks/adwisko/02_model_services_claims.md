# 02 - Model, Services and Claims

## What they offer (from their site)
1. Profile-based **university shortlisting** ("1 lakh+ courses"), "dedicated Academic Advisor".
2. **Application handling** for "all major destinations"; no limit on number of universities (advise <=8).
3. **SOP help** by an "Editorial team".
4. **Scholarship Advisors**: an "AI-powered scholarship search engine" matches the profile; advisor applies to **5 scholarships per university**.
5. **Education loan assistance via WeMakeScholars** (no WMS service fee).
6. Add-ons: "lowest forex rates", budget accommodation deals (referral-type services; providers not named).
7. "100% digital process".

## Who pays them? (not disclosed)
- Students: "100% free", "No commissions. No pressure." (LinkedIn, 31 Jan 2026).
- But T&C: students may pay **university application fees through Adwisko** by UPI/NEFT/RTGS/IMPS; "no payment gateway integrated"; Adwisko "will not have any role" in disputes; **no refund for third-party payments** such as university application fee or "any study abroad related service fee". The phrase "study abroad related service fee" implies fees other than application fees may be collected; none are listed.
- Plausible funding sources (hypotheses, none confirmed):
  1. Commission/partnership payments from universities (the standard agent model). A Reddit commenter describes the common model and says reputable universities do not pay agents; weak universities pay more. Not specific evidence about Adwisko.
  2. Cross-subsidy from the founders' WMS business: loan referrals pay WMS, which funds Adwisko as a lead generator. The Adwisko -> WMS loan link makes this very likely.
  3. Forex/accommodation/insurance referral fees.
  4. Scholarship-provider/CSR arrangements.
- Consequence: free does not mean neutral. The advisors' incentives (and company revenue) probably depend on a student enrolling and borrowing through WMS. Ask for disclosure.

## Loan-conditional "scholarships" in the group (relevant to what counts as a "scholarship")
WMS publishes university pages "recommended" for WMS loans, e.g.:
- **Jonkoping University**: 30% tuition waiver for Indian bachelor/master students with 60% GPA who **"arrange the remaining funds through an Education Loan approved by WeMakeScholars"**.
- **Duke Law (LLM)**: USD 20,000 for two students who obtain loan approval through WMS.
If Adwisko's "scholarships won" count includes these (not confirmed), then the headline success rate partly reflects loan-linked benefits that require borrowing via the founders' other company. Ask Adwisko to break down: how many were unconditional merit awards, how many required a loan through WMS.

## Red flags summary
1. "Guaranteed admission/funding" wording.
2. Payments for university applications collected via Adwisko's own account, non-refundable, no gateway.
3. Stats that cannot be audited, with profile screening.
4. Revenue source undisclosed while claiming "no commissions" and "100% free".
5. Same founders also own the lender-referral partner; conflicts of interest not stated.
6. Very thin paid-up capital (INR 1 lakh) behind promises of guarantees and refunds.
7. Government/CSR authority language that cannot be traced.
