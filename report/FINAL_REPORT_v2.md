## 1. Bottom line
1. **Curriculum fit (my 0-28.5 skills score for a gameplay-systems / game-AI programmer, [R31]):** DigiPen 93% > Abertay 88% > **ISSE 81%** > **Aizu 75%** = **h_da 75%** > DIT 60% = TH Köln 60%. (v1: UWA 61%, Deakin 49%.)
2. **Among options that are affordable, ISSE and Aizu are the best curricula and are close.** The 6-point gap is smaller than my scoring error. ISSE has a game-AI lab (ICE Lab, Prof. Thawonmas) and Osaka/Kyoto next to Capcom/Nintendo. Aizu has the more game-relevant course list I could see (C++ course, OpenGL/GLSL graphics, physics animation, Unity/VR/game-engine course, AIMA-style AI) at about **Rs 50 lakh vs Rs 82 lakh**.
3. **Abertay and DigiPen have the best curricula but are priced wrong** for a loan-funded student (Rs 119-140 lakh; Rs 283 lakh) [R51]. They are benchmarks, not recommendations.
4. **Germany is now a legitimate fallback**: h_da Animation & Game (Technology) is the best German fit for games plus AI; DIT Artificial Intelligence is the best AI curriculum. Cost Rs 62-80 lakh, but degree arrives around 2032, needs German B1-B2, and he said he disliked the "DIY" feel.
5. **A Master's is not required for game programming jobs in Japan** (see v1 [R04]); the thesis lab is his portfolio. So the best bachelor's is one that gives maths + systems + a strong lab, not necessarily the most expensive.

## 5. How much will the degree matter? (updated opinion)
- Unchanged: the degree's main value for an Indian wanting to work abroad is the **work-rights pathway plus structured fundamentals** (maths, algorithms, systems); specific courses age fast. Japanese game studios hire new graduates on portfolio, coding tests and Japanese ability; a Master's is not required.
- New: because ISSE and Aizu are similar on curriculum, **the extra Rs 32 lakh for ISSE must be justified by the lab, the Osaka/Kyoto location and campus life**, not by the course list. For a loan-funded family, the cheaper good curriculum wins unless the ICE Lab access is confirmed.
- AI risk is unchanged: the hedge is breadth (systems, evaluation, hardware limits) and a public portfolio. Both programmes leave room to build it.
