# Are his interests worth pursuing long term? (AI era) - opinion + evidence, 2026-10-25

**Short answer:** Yes - but point them at the transferable skill stack (systems/C++, maths, graphics/simulation, ML, hardware-software integration), not at "game AI" alone. Game AI is the riskiest label; the skills underneath are the durable part.

## Evidence (searched 2026-10-25)
- **Game industry (GDC 2026 State of the Industry, 2,300+ respondents):** 28% laid off in the past 2 years (33% in the US); 48% of those still looking for work; half say their employer cut staff in the past year; 74% of surveyed students worry about job prospects (few junior roles, competition from laid-off veterans, AI displacement); game designers had the highest layoff rate (20% in 12 months); 36% use generative AI, 52% think it harms the industry (game programming: 59% negative). Sources: https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/ ; https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/survey-one-in-four-developers-laid-off-over-the-past-two-years ; https://files.gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/state-of-the-game-industry-2026.pdf
- **Entry-level software jobs:** employment of software developers aged 22-25 down nearly 20% since 2024 (Stanford AI Index via CNBC); postings recovering (+~15% since Feb 2025) but ~27.5% below pre-pandemic, and 71% of the recent increase is senior roles; demand shifting to AI, cloud, systems, integration. Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/11/ai-software-developer-jobs.html . SignalFire: new-grad hiring down ~65% at big tech vs 2019; engineers among the least affected by "AI code" displacement; https://www.signalfire.com/blog/signalfire-state-of-talent-report-2026 . AP: CS grads face a tougher market: https://apnews.com/article/ai-computer-science-jobs-college-degree-99416cc8981d701e3674cf3dfa501491 . Adobe/LinkedIn talent report: AI Engineer share of hires 14x 2019; fewer CS grads start in SWE, more in adjacent roles.
- **Robotics / physical AI:** ~5,000 open roles on one job board (robotics, autonomy, perception, controls, simulation, integration with hardware limits); engineering-led, includes new-grad roles. Source: https://www.physicalai.jobs/reports/state-of-physical-ai-jobs-2026 (single job board; a signal, not a market study).
- **Limits:** data is mostly US; Japan's fresher-recruitment system and Germany's market not checked (my guess: Japan's system cushions the entry-level squeeze).

## Opinion
1. His real interest (memory-aware, hardware-limited, adaptive systems) maps to robotics, edge/embedded AI, simulation, real-time systems and AI infrastructure. AI automates routine code more than performance/hardware/system-design reasoning.
2. Games alone are a fragile career bet (layoffs, scarce junior roles). Fine as a first target or side path; keep an exit.
3. Do not choose a major named after a trend ("AI", "game design"). Game design is the weakest option. Fundamentals + one deep specialty age best.
4. A degree still matters as a filter (I believe visa routes like the German Blue Card require one - not verified here).

## Majors and minors
- **Major:** Computer Science (or CS with an AI track), leaning systems/maths/graphics rather than web/app development.
- **Strong alternative:** Computer or Electrical Engineering, or robotics-flavoured CS (adds hardware; harder to automate).
- **Minors:** maths/statistics (probability, optimisation, linear algebra); electronics/control/robotics.
- **Keep options open:** KAIST allows choosing a major after year 1; check electives for robotics/embedded/simulation at ISSE and Aizu (NOT yet checked).
